How it works

  1. Start LANcelot

    Double-click on the classroom PC. The teacher screen opens on the board.

  2. Students scan

    They scan the QR code or type the address. Enter a name and done.

  3. Play and learn

    You pick the quiz and the game. The class plays along, you follow everything live.

Five halls, one way of working

Every part has an icon and one sentence of explanation. Know one hall and you know them all.

  • Play

    Practise through play with your own quizzes: quick quizzes, duels, team games and adventures.

  • Poll

    Ask the whole class: polls, statements, word clouds, open questions and circle time.

  • Create

    Make quizzes, worksheets and tests, with AI or by hand. Play them live or print them with an answer key.

  • Class tools

    On the board: pick a student, groups, timer, noise meter, traffic light, dice, class jobs and seating plan.

  • My class

    Follow your class: tasks and tests per student, marks, support and your country's standards.

Working with AI

Give a topic and the grade, or drop in a photo of your textbook. The AI makes a quiz, a worksheet, a test or a whole series per lesson. You check it, adjust it and play.

  • Quiz: questions with explanations, ready to play in the Arena.
  • Worksheet: on three levels, live in class or printed with an answer sheet.
  • Series per lesson: choose your subject and lessons, LANcelot makes a quiz and a worksheet for each lesson.
  • Test: let the AI make one, or drop in your own paper test and it becomes digital. Then you finish it with buttons.
  • Works with Gemini, ChatGPT or Claude: LANcelot copies the prompt, you paste the answer back. Or enter your own key and everything happens in one click.
  • Only creating with AI needs internet. Playing runs over the classroom network.

Follow-up, support and marks

My class opens on Follow-up: every student next to every task and test. The home screen shows what is ready for today and what you still need to mark.

  • One overview

    For each student you see at once what is finished, what is still in progress and what went wrong. Anyone who was absent or doesn't take the subject doesn't count.

  • Support overview

    Students who need the most attention are at the top. See which goals the whole class should revisit, who needs small-group instruction and which mistakes keep coming back.

  • Tasks per student

    Give a quiz, worksheet or test to one student, a group or the whole class, each at their own level. Right away, at the start of the lesson or on a day and time you choose.

  • Marks and retakes

    Test marks go straight into a mark list, out of 10, 20 or 100, ready for your report. A retake takes one button, and you choose what counts.

  • Goals for your country

    Choose the curriculum goals of England, the US, the Netherlands, Germany, France or Flanders, or add your own. An AI can suggest them. Working without goals is fine too.

  • Prepare at home

    Link My lesson folder to your own OneDrive or Google Drive. What you set up at home is on the board the next morning, with the same tasks, tests and results.

Tests on the Chromebook or on paper

Build your test with buttons, no code. Students take it on their device or on paper. LANcelot marks what it can, you check the rest and the marks appear in your overview straight away.

  • Multiple choice, true or false, fill in, matching, ordering, sorting, grouping, highlighting in a text and dictation.
  • Maths too: drawing on a grid, measuring with a protractor, polygons and calculation grids that mark themselves.
  • Read aloud per question or per student, slower if needed, and listening questions with your own audio clip.
  • Try the test yourself as a student first, and watch live while the class works.
  • After marking, each student gets a PDF in your lesson folder. If you like, it is emailed automatically to a support teacher or the parents, from your own mailbox.

Student data stays in your classroom

  • LANcelot runs on your own PC. There is no LANcelot cloud and no student accounts.
  • Names, answers and results live in a folder on that PC. You decide what happens to them.
  • No ads and no tracking.
  • If you use AI, only your prompt (topic or lesson material) goes to the AI you choose. No student names.
  • You switch on My lesson folder and emailing tests yourself. Your lesson folder then goes through your own OneDrive or Google Drive and the emails through your own mailbox. That uses the internet.

Frequently asked questions

When can I use LANcelot?

LANcelot is coming soon to the Microsoft Store, with a trial month in which everything works. This website is the first place to hear when it is ready.

What does LANcelot cost?

A one-time €29.99, yours to keep. First you try everything for a month without paying. No subscription and no cost per pupil.

Does LANcelot work with my country's goals?

Yes. In My class, choose your country's curriculum goals, add your own, or work without goals. Every answer then lands under the right goal in your overview.

Do I need the internet?

Playing runs over the school's local network: the PC and the students' devices need to be on the same network. The internet is only needed for what you switch on yourself: creating material with AI, My lesson folder through OneDrive or Google Drive, and emailing tests.

Can I prepare at home and use more than one PC?

Yes. Link My lesson folder to your OneDrive or Google Drive, at home and on the classroom PC. Quizzes, tests, tasks and results are then on both PCs, and a task you schedule at home is ready on the board the next morning.

Do students need an account?

No. They type their name and play. LANcelot remembers their knight and results on the classroom PC.

How many students can play?

There is no limit. A whole class of thirty plays smoothly, on Chromebooks, tablets and phones mixed.

What do I need?

A Windows PC (usually the one connected to the board), the school network and devices with a browser for the students.

Can I use my own lesson content?

Of course. Write quizzes yourself, let the AI make them from your own textbook or PDF, and sort everything into folders per subject and lesson.
